02The problem

One band. Many emitters. All at once.

In contested spectrum, friendly links, radars and deliberate interference overlap in time and frequency. An operator has seconds to work out what is on the air. NEXA answers three questions for every emitter it finds.

What

What is it?

One of 60 signal types, or the family when the exact type cannot be told apart.

How sure

How sure is it?

A confidence band on every answer: high, medium or uncertain.

Where

Where and when?

A box on the spectrogram: its frequency, start time and duration.

06How it works

See the whole spectrum, then name every emitter.

1 · See the spectrum

1Capture

Raw IQ samples from a radio receiver, a recorded file or the simulator. Seconds of the whole band at once.

2Spectrogram

The band as a picture: time across, frequency down. Every emitter leaves its own mark.

3Detector

A YOLO model draws a box around every emitter in time and frequency, many at once.

4Cut and clean

Each box is filtered and cut into 160 µs windows at its own frequency; one per pulse for pulsed signals.

2 · Name it

5Classifier

Multi-View Physics-Fusion Network: raw IQ, spectrogram and spectrum CNNs fused with physics features.

6Tracker and vote

Joins one emitter's windows over time and averages them; a new signal in the band gets its own track.

CW radar / tone jam ?LoRa / swept jam ?jam on signal reactiveQAM size group

7Resolver

Settles look-alikes from behaviour over time, e.g. a jammer switching on over a signal is reactive.

8Dashboard

Boxes on the spectrogram and one row per emitter: answer, confidence band, frequency, time, alerts.
